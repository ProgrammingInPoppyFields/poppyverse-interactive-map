Who are you?

H.A.H is the pseudonym of this blog’s author.
By daylight: a product designer + engineer.
By starlight: the kind of person who dreams of programming 30-foot mechs optimized for emotional support, deadpan sarcasm, and the occasional high-yield detonation.

The name also happens to share initials with Helen A. He.

And yes, H.A.H sounds suspiciously like a rogue comedy AI having an identity crisis.
This is not entirely a coincidence.

Is this GenAI?

—Yeah. Obviously.
You probably guessed from the em dashes and the unearned confidence.

But let’s be specific.

AI helps draft, expand, remix, and stitch things together. The characters, worlds, obsessions, plot decisions, visual language, and frankly alarming amount of accumulated lore come from the human behind the keyboard.

The human lays down the dots.
The AI plays connect-the-trauma.

Sometimes it finds a connection I didn’t expect. Sometimes it produces something brilliant. Sometimes it produces absolute sludge.

Then I cut, redirect, rewrite, regenerate, rearrange, argue with it, steal one good sentence from twelve bad ones, and keep going until the thing sounds like it belongs here.

So yes: AI-assisted.
But somebody still has to decide what matters.

And if something hits a little too precisely—
if it feels too quiet, too strange, too true—
don’t ask what the model was trained on.

Ask who told it where to look.

What the hell is lucid generation?

A dramatic name for a pretty simple process:

The machine generates.
The human stays awake.

Nothing here runs on autopilot. Ideas get prompted, redirected, rejected, rewritten, stitched together, contradicted, resurrected, and occasionally dragged back from somewhere deeply stupid.

It’s less “press button, receive story” and more an ongoing conversation with a machine that is very good at pattern-making and has absolutely no idea why any of this matters.

That part is still the human’s job.

So: guided hallucination.
With fingerprints.
And somebody still awake in the middle of it.
